Trust in a security platform is not a compliance badge you earn once and frame.
It is the answer to a specific question: *when the platform made this decision,
what was it looking at, and can you prove it?*

Most platforms cannot. The decision was made against a state that has since
changed, against a policy version that has since shipped, by a model whose
assumptions were never written down. The audit becomes a reconstruction, and
the reconstruction is partial.

**Meridian** holds the lineage. Every decision the platform makes — an access
allow, a path classification, a trust score — is recorded against the exact graph
state and policy version that produced it. The audit is not reconstructed later;
it is captured at the moment the decision happens.

That is what “audit-ready by construction” means. The platform does not become
trustworthy by claiming to be. It becomes trustworthy because every assertion it
makes is verifiable, after the fact, against the record it kept.
